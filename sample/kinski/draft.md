First thing to understand about Rikers is the doors don’t work. I mean the cell doors do not lock. Why? There are a few reasons. One: Inmates make what they call ‘Keys.’ These consist of a thin piece of bedsheet torn into a strip. The locking mechanism is essentially a heavy duty version of a hook & an eye. So perps'll tie the sheet strip around the hook, the latch. You can’t see it from the outside. When they want out they simply pull up on it and it unlocks. Some use soap whittled down and stuffed into the eye mechanism. These are easier to pull open from the outside. Then there are sliding doors that when you play with them enough, just rocking back and forth, they swing open because the track they are on is just worn the fuck out. It can be creepy when you are on overnight tour in the dark, locked in with people who may be facing life and have nothing to lose just decide they want out.

The Bureau of Corrections, civilians, I’ve only interacted with them a few times, they work with the Mayor, and they can at any time walk into a jail and show their ID and you have to let them in, few people have that privilege. I’m walking one time and it’s early morning, 5, 6, and one of them shows up and wants to walk down the tier with me, and suddenly a guy sticks his head out, the guy from the Bureau’s fuckin terrified, and the prisoner closes the door like My bad! And I say, Yeah a lotta the doors don’t work and he says We know that. Everybody knows man. It’s like the story Zizek used to tell, Stalin’s drinking, everyone’s drinking round the table, and someone stands up and says, You’re a liar you’re a murderer you’re this and that, you know. And another guy stands up and responds, How dare you say that to the General Secretary, you can’t say that here. And you know, Zizek’s point is, That second guy? Who calls the first guy out? Used to be they’d split em up, you know. Five Crips, five Bloods, five Latin Kings, five Trinitarios, five neutrals. Keep the balance of power.  Nowadays they let em all live together, keep all their cats with them. These guys they know your fuckin body clock better than you do. They’re there 18 hours, they know when you’re gonna get a coffee, fill your water bottle, piss, you know. 

Now, a lot of people confuse jails and prisons. Rikers is a jail, it’s where you go while you’re waiting for trial. Average stay is six months or under. People bail out, plead out. It’s a game, waiting for trial. Some people try to stretch it out, delay delay delay. Some people are trying to stay in the jail system cuz it’s all they know. Rikers, as far as jails go, is much better than prison. Once you go to prison, the state owns you. You’re not a name you’re a number that’s it, and you’ll die up there. But at Rikers, you’re—technically—innocent til proven guilty, which means you’ve got more rights. 

Rikers is also a direct supervision environment. That means it's you and your body in the same room as the inmates. You're not sitting in another room watching cameras, sitting in a bubble behind glass. There are cameras, to be sure, but they're there for legal, they're there for the city, they're there to protect the inmates.

Guys are coming in and out of houses, there’s a rotating cast, new Crips coming in and out. Someone’s always running the house. Shotcallers. That’s the head inmate, runs the house. I mean, in theory the prison runs the house but you know. The shotcallers’d keep their guys in check sometimes, tell their guys, Cut it out, cuz if this turns up they gonna tear this house apart, and you know what they got, drugs n shit. They don’t want the tactical search team in there. So sometimes you can hold guys off when they’re bein difficult, saner minds prevail, you know? In everybody’s interest to keep the heat off. 

So when you're new to a house, you've gotta figure out who’s the leader. You always ask so you know who to talk to, if you’re gonna be there for a week. Just to say, I’m not gonna fuck with you, I certainly didn’t put you here, but I’m gonna do my job for the week I’m here, and tough if you don’t like it. They’ll test you too, send a minion, see how you react. They’ll throw your logbook on the floor, move a chair around, see how you play it. If they sense someone’s real scared, they’ll shark-attack em, get all around em and scream in their face. Intimidation games. Rookies’ll quit on the spot sometimes, that happens. 

Point being that nowadays, you’re in an MS13 house, or a Blood house, and one of em swings at you, and you swing back? Now you swung on the whole group. Whole club’s involved. Story is the Hells Angels started it. You’re not swingin on the person you’re swingin on the patch and you’re looking to catch a beat down. Goddamn MS13 almost killed me, ripped my head open, I lose a tooth, surgery for months. Still have a hit on me those guys. Barbarians. 

A lot's changed since I started. Island’s three times bigger than it used to be. Landfilled with rocks 'n the crushed bones of the fuckin Lenape. Dug and dumped for the subway tunnels right through a burial ground.

Now they wanna extend the La Guardia runways, land their fuckin Airbus Flying Palaces, you know; these are the people who run the world. They wanna privatize the jails. I was working the gate, thirty people come through from the Manhattan Institute lookin like they just came outta Yale, young n suits n shit, they wanna see a warden. Now the Manhattan Institute’s _raison d’être_ is to privatize shit, you know what I mean? So everyone in power, they want the jail to fail. They could make the place safer but they don’t wanna, they just paint the walls, so at quarterly meetings they can say they’re spendin all this money but nothin’s happenin, so alright folks, looks like it’s cheaper just to tear her down, build a new one, and the tax payers’ on the hook.

When I started out there was crazy shit everywhere, I mean nothing can prepare you, and I’d ask other COs you know, veterans, Yo how do you deal with this shit? They’d say, you gotta make this job work for you. As you do it you start to understand, you gotta develop your own style. Nothing can prepare you. The best officers, their style works maybe 70% of the time. The average ones, 40% of the time. The ones it works 10% of the time either move up the ranks and become a captain, or else they leave, cuz they can’t cut it on the floor.  

Captains would handle alerts totally differently man to man. A Code B alert means there's some shit going down, someone has a weapon or has made a threat. If a Code B goes off all the guys in the lunch room, they call it the keepers kitchen. That’s where officers would be hanging out, COs used to be called keepers back in the day. But all the guys, you'd look around the room put together a team of guys who can throw, because half of em can't, they're 90lbs, so you look around and say, alright, who's our muscle, and then you head to the house to throw down. Sometimes the issue’s easily rectifiable, you know, guys were supposed to get bananas with their lunch and they didn’t, and they saw other guys down the hall eating bananas. Sometimes it’s just guys looking for trouble over nothin. And captains have very different styles how they respond. Sometimes the captain'll stand there and argue with guys. Try to talk em down. Sometimes the captain's of the belief that might makes right. And he figures—not totally wrongly either—that if the COs working your house called in a problem? If they can’t talk you down, the guys who know you best? Then what the fuck am I gonna say to change your mind? So they’d just go right in swinging. Sometimes it was a mess and you don’t have time to talk, I mean you’re on your lunch break you’re already missing your lunch break and you’re suited up, gas mask on, baton in your hand, and you get the order to just gas up the whole house, like, let’s end this. It was war. Everyone’s gettin it. I remember this guy loved to spray motherfuckers, he carried three OCs, bear spray and then two backups in his belt, he’d just hose em down. Sometimes the inmates see your team show up in suits, they say naw, today’s not the today, and they settle down. 

They can teach you all the rules and regs in Academy but when you’re dealing with a house and it’s 30 or 50 guys, 30 or 50 hurtin motherfuckers you know what I mean? Their life is about getting one over. Every interaction you have, the way they see it, every interaction someone’s getting played. Everyone is either a predator or prey in this situation. And it could be something, it could be the smallest thing, on the micro level, a couple packets of sugar. These fuckers will fight over a fuckin cups worth of instant coffee. Somethin to do cuz you’re climbin the walls, something to just keep your mind occupied fighting over all this shit. 

Not to mention, the Academy leaves half the important shit out, it's all theory, zero zilch practice. The perps are the ones help orient you, they've been around, they know the drill, if they're in the mood, they'll tell you. It's a collaboration almost, sometimes, keeping out of trouble, keeping everyone, inmates, officers, low profile. No one wants a raid, no one wants a lockdown. 

My personal style? Is respect. We’re all stuck in here together. I’ll treat you like an adult til you prove me different. We’ve all got our roles in the system. You give me a hard day I’ll give you a hard day, but I’m not here to fuck with you. Guys’ll put on appearances for captains they respect, they’ll know how to act, keep the doors closed when captain walks by. 

One of the reasons I was able to retire early. I was never what they call a Robo Cop, this big muscular dude who says, Make no mistake I’m in charge here. Those guys can get jumped just so they an inmate can get one, over win points for taking him out, put a target on his back. But like I said, I’m more a dude to give respect. In a max house, fuckin murderer’s row, guys who’re never gonna see the streets again, or guys who just got a DUI, I treat em all the same. You used to sit in a plastic chair with your logbook, now we got desks, kinda like in the old movies when they go into a hotel, big hardbound thing, ledger lines, and I always just did my job. And on that job if you’re doing your job you’re already a step above. You’re doing too much. Most guys are fuckin goin to sleep. Yeah you heard that right. Guys sleep in the pocket, hang out on their phones, gossip. That’s how you get through 22 years without any problems. The more you work the more you’re gonna get worked. You know what I mean? That’s the idealism. I was tryna make a difference, my credo was, I’m responsible for the effort not the outcome. That’s how I lived my life. But then I realized, these guys, the Department, they can take your job at any moment. They’d love to, it’d make their day. 

There’s all this paperwork but it’s just a setup. You go into the bubble where the camera can’t see you, you pull ten guys’ cards, and you write a report saying no contraband. My Captain, he told me to do this. Straight up. It’s crazy.

It's all about the cameras. Half the job is acting right for the cameras. Inmates, COs. The Feds technically own the cameras. Lemme tell you a little story about performing for the camera. 2014 was when the Feds ordered cameras to be installed in all jails. It was a slow job getting them installed. We had to adapt along with the changes. For instance. The incident with MS13. When I came back from the hospital. This is a few years later, 2017. Every officer I saw came up hugged me and whispered “You already know.” See, there were cameras in the housing area I was assaulted in. By that time, in pretty much every cell area and most of the dorms, there were cameras. But not in the main clinic or the mental health clinic and some parts of intake, due to HIPAA laws. During roll call, the good managers would be like, “Look, you know you’re on camera. Those cameras are not there to protect you,” which, damn right. No, “They are there to protect the inmates. So, be aware and make that movie. Step back put your hand up and spray em.” The reason for this? Well, protocol dictates that any inmate exposed to chemical agent must be examined in the medical clinic in timely fashion. See where I’m going with this? No cameras in there. That’s where we got our payback. In my case, I was told in no uncertain terms that after I EMS’d out and the guys who jumped me were in a holding cell in the clinic? But much later, and not walking under their own power.

It’s premodern, pre-law. _Blood Meridian_ shit, frontier justice. Wild West system built on honor and respect, violence and fear. The public front is that it's oh so civilized, just another enlightened bureaucracy, but we know the truth. Sink or swim. Predator or prey. Rikers is a dinosaur stuck in a tar-pit. A crossroads of the way things are and the way things were. But society? Doesn't wanna know. It can't handle the truth.
